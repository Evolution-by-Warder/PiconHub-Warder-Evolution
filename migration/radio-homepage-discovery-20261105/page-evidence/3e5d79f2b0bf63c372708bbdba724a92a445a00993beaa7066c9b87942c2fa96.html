Ottawa voted Monday: CKCU podcast puts youth voices in the spotlight

Ottawa voters headed to the polls Monday after months of campaigning by candidates hoping to shape the city’s future.

At CKCU 93.1 FM, Elxn26 Ottawa gives listeners a chance to get to know mayoral candidates Jeff Leiper, Neil Saravanamuttoo, and Alex Lawson, along with other candidates running across the city.

Incumbent Mayor Mark Sutcliffe was invited but did not return our calls.

Hosted by Hue, a Lisgar Collegiate Institute student and Ottawa-Carleton District School Board student trustee, the youth-focused podcast explores candidate platforms, local issues, and civic engagement.

Inspired by conversations with friends too young to vote, Hue shows that young people can still shape their city’s future.

On Monday, make your voice heard. Get out and vote!

Listen at elxnottawa.ca and support Hue’s work and community media by donating to their CKCU fundraising campaign.

CKCU’s 2026 Funding Drive is Coming: Keep Media Human

CKCU’s annual Funding Drive runs from October 23 to November 8, and we’re asking listeners and supporters to help us raise $215,000 for independent, community-powered media.

This year’s message is simple: Keep Media Human.

In a world of misinformation, fake news, and AI-generated content, trust and genuine human connections matter more than ever. We believe in media shaped by real people, music chosen with care, and stories and conversations that reflect our friends, families and neighbours.

For 51 years, CKCU has connected volunteers, artists, listeners and communities through music, culture, news and ideas. Independent community media provides space for diverse voices, meaningful conversations and perspectives that might otherwise go unheard.

Your support helps protect that space and keep it growing.

CKCU’s Chris White to Receive Folk Canada Lifetime Achievement Award

CKCU producer and host Chris White will receive the 2026 Estelle Klein Lifetime Achievement Award from Folk Canada at its annual conference in Hamilton on October 14. The award recognizes Chris’s decades of contributions to folk music and community arts in Ottawa and beyond.

Named after Estelle Klein, who served as artistic director of the Mariposa Folk Festival from 1964 to 1980, the award honours a lasting contribution to the folk music community. Klein’s approach to programming had a major influence on Chris, particularly in his work with the Ottawa Folk Festival.

Chris co-founded the Ottawa Folk Festival in 1993 with Max Wallace, who was CKCU’s Station Director at the time. As artistic director, Chris programmed and shaped the festival for 16 years, developing a welcoming, participatory atmosphere that brought people together through music, singing, dance and creative workshops.

His influence on Ottawa’s music community extends well beyond the festival. Chris has helped organize numerous community music events, supported songwriters and performers, and led singing groups for people of all ages and abilities. He is also a songwriter and guitarist who has released three albums of original music, and has worked as a teacher, concert producer and choir director.

Chris has been producing and hosting Canadian Spaces, CKCU’s long-running folk and roots music program, since 2013. He also helps high school students develop radio and video skills through co-op placements at the station, sharing his knowledge and enthusiasm with a new generation.

Folk Canada Executive Director Rosalyn Dennett praised Chris’s decades of work across festivals, radio and songwriting communities, noting the lasting impact of his contributions to folk music in Ontario and beyond.

Dennett will interview Chris about his musical life on ReFolkUs. The interview will be broadcast and streamed on CKCU this Monday, October 12, at 7 a.m. Eastern. It will also air on CFBX FM in Kamloops on Friday at 7 a.m. Pacific and on CKUW FM in Winnipeg on Sunday at 3 p.m. Central. You can also listen to it on Amazon Music.

The interview will be available on Folk Canada’s website and on several podcast platforms.

Congratulations to Chris on this well-deserved recognition of his many years of bringing people together through music!

Read the Folk Canada announcement for more about this year’s award recipients.

People of CKCU: Maia Fraser

“Finding a Community”

From a high school podcast project to teaching the next generation of radio makers, Maia Fraser’s journey at CKCU has opened doors, introduced her to a new community, and helped shape her plans for the future.

Maia first connected with CKCU through a school passion project called What the Actual Job, a podcast featuring interviews with people about their careers.
